Drawing by Drawing

The Complete Drawing Set

We produce every drawing in sequence — each one building on the last, forming a complete and consistent set your contractor can work from without confusion.

1
📐
Layout Design
The layout drawing is the big-picture plan of your entire plot — showing the building's position on the site, setback distances from boundaries, vehicle access, compound wall, and open spaces. Before any rooms are drawn, we establish exactly where the building sits and what space is used for what purpose.

This is the drawing that goes to the Planning Authority first (PPA) and sets the parameters for everything that follows.
Plot Boundary Setback Dimensions Access & Parking North Direction
2
🏠
Floor Plan
The floor plan is the most-used drawing on site. It shows every room, wall, door, window, staircase, and opening — viewed from above as if the roof is removed. Every dimension is marked; room sizes, wall thicknesses, opening widths, and overall building dimensions.

We produce a separate plan for each floor — e.g. ground, first, second, and terrace. The floor plan is the single most important drawing for the structural engineer, contractor, and client.
Room Dimensions Wall Thicknesses Door & Window Schedule Per Floor
3
🏛️
Elevation Drawing
Elevations show the external face of the building from each side — front, rear, left, and right. These drawings show the height of the building, floor-to-floor levels, roof line, window and door positions as they appear from outside, and external finishes.

Elevations are essential for planning submission, material selection (which tile, cladding, or plaster finish goes where), and giving your client a realistic picture of how the finished building will look before construction begins.
4 Elevations Floor-Level Heights Roof Profile External Finishes
4
✂️
Section Drawing
A section drawing is like slicing the building in half and looking inside — it reveals what floor-to-ceiling heights, staircase geometry, beam depths, slab thickness, and foundation depth look like in vertical cross-section.

Sections are critical for the structural engineer to size beams and slabs correctly. They also show how split-level changes, staircases, and double-height spaces work in reality — things that are invisible on a floor plan.
Floor-to-Floor Height Slab Thickness Beam Depth Staircase Detail
5
🗺️
Site Plan
The site plan is more detailed than the initial layout — it shows the finished ground levels, drainage outlets, rainwater pipes, compound gate position, septic tank or sump location, overhead tank, service connections (electricity, water, sewer), and landscape areas.

This drawing is required for PPA / local body sanction and is used by civil engineers to plan earthworks, drainage, and utility connections before the building starts.
Contour Levels Drainage Layout Service Connections CMDA Submission
6
⬛
Foundation & Frame Planning
This is the structural drawing set — showing column positions (the grid), footing sizes and depths, plinth beam layout, tie beam positions, and slab reinforcement details. It is prepared in coordination with the structural engineer.

It specifies rebar size, spacing, cover, and lap lengths — ensuring the structural frame is exactly what the engineering calculations require. This drawing directly controls construction cost and safety.
Column Grid Footing Sizes Rebar Schedule Plinth Beam Layout
7
⚡
MEP Point Plan
MEP stands for Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing. This drawing overlays all service point positions onto the floor plan — every light point, socket, switch board, AC slot, fan point, water inlet, drain point, and appliance connection is marked with its exact location and type.

Without an MEP drawing, electricians and plumbers work from memory or guesswork — leading to incorrect conduit routing, missed points, and walls that need to be broken after plastering. We mark every point before the slab is cast, so nothing is missed.
Electrical Points Plumbing Inlets Drain Outlets AC & Exhaust Switch Board Layout
